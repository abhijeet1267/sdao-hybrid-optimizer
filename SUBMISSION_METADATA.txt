[3] SUBMISSION ABSTRACT  (target: < 250 words; actual: 248 words)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Vector databases increasingly support hybrid SQL queries that combine
relational predicates (e.g., WHERE price < 100) with approximate
nearest-neighbour (ANN) top-k over high-dimensional embeddings. A
latency-driven query planner will lead with the exact-SQL scan in
selective predicates and the ANN index scan in non-selective ones,
yet the recall of the latter can silently drop to 0.10-0.22 in
production, producing wrong top-k results. We formalise this choice
as a recall-constrained admission problem and prove a data-dependent
(epsilon, delta)-PAC bound on the per-bucket mean Recall@10 of each
candidate plan. The bound is computed once per (strategy,
selectivity-bucket) pair offline, then evaluated in O(1) inside a
PostgreSQL planner hook that gates every query. We validate it
empirically on a 200,000-row SIFT-128 audit and a full 1,000,000-row
production benchmark (cold-cache dynamics, q-error injection, PAC
verification across 30 random seeds). The headline finding is
structural: hybrid ANN plans deliver a 40-65x latency reduction over
the SQL scan, but Recall@10 is bounded away from 0.95 by the fixed
ef_search=100 graph-traversal budget, not by planner tuning. The
admission gate therefore routes 100% of production-quality
(R_target >= 0.9) queries to the exact-SQL plan in the 1M
environment, admitting hybrid plans only for R_target <= 0.3
(RAG surface-and-rerank workloads). The 200K audit shows the gate
admits hybrid plans at 80% when the buffer cache and selectivity
are favourable, proving the boundary is data-driven rather than
hard-coded. Across 20 PAC cells the empirical Bernstein CI
satisfies the theoretical guarantee (pass rate 1.00 >= 1 - delta
= 0.95).
